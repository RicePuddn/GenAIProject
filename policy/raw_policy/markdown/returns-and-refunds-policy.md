# Returns and Refunds Policy

Effective date: 29 October 2026.

## 1. Application for returns and refunds

Subject to the conditions in this Policy, customers may apply for the return of purchased goods and/or a refund. A request may concern goods received by the customer or goods that have not been received. Submission of a request does not, by itself, constitute acceptance of the return or approval of a refund.

This Policy applies to physical goods purchased in Singapore dollars for delivery within Singapore. The same standard conditions apply to all customer loyalty tiers.

All dates are assessed in Singapore time. Calendar days include weekends and public holidays. Where a period is stated in business days, Saturdays, Sundays and Singapore public holidays are excluded. A specific exception changes only the condition expressly identified; the remaining conditions continue to apply.

## 2. Application for the return of goods

### 2.1 Goods received by the customer

A customer may submit a return request within thirty (30) calendar days after delivery of the affected goods. The delivery date is day zero, and a request submitted on the thirtieth day is within the ordinary request period. The period runs from actual delivery, not the order date, payment date or dispatch date.

Where an order is delivered in separate parcels, each affected item's period runs from delivery of the parcel containing that item. Delivery of another parcel from the same order does not shorten the period. If an item's delivery date or parcel allocation cannot be established, the request will require clarification or human review.

The date the request was first recorded determines whether it was submitted on time. A timely request does not become late because inspection or review takes place later. However, timely submission does not waive the conditions relating to evidence, item condition or return shipment. For example, goods delivered on 5 October have an ordinary request deadline of 4 November, inclusive.

Requests outside the ordinary return period are addressed in section 2.6.

### 2.2 Goods not received by the customer

A customer may raise a non-receipt claim where:

(i) the estimated delivery date has passed and the parcel has not arrived;

(ii) tracking shows delivery but the customer has not received the parcel; or

(iii) the carrier has confirmed that the parcel was lost or damaged in transit.

Non-receipt claims require investigation of the shipment status, delivery records and any carrier findings. A non-receipt claim does not require the customer to return goods that they do not possess. A refund remains subject to the investigation outcome and the conditions in section 6 of this Policy.

### 2.3 Reasons for a return request

A return and/or refund may be requested in the following circumstances:

(a) the parcel is empty or the quantity received is less than the quantity purchased;

(b) supplied accessories or components are missing;

(c) the goods differ from the purchased item or variant, such as a different model, size or colour;

(d) the goods are defective or do not function as intended;

(e) the goods sustained physical damage before or during delivery; or

(f) the customer wishes to return goods in their original, unused condition due to a change of mind.

The reason selected must correspond to the issue reported. A concern that goods may be counterfeit is subject to human review under section 7.3 rather than an automatic refund or rejection. Before goods are treated as missing, any outstanding parcels from the same order must be taken into account.

### 2.4 Change-of-mind returns

A change-of-mind return is available within the period in section 2.1 only where the goods:

(i) are unused and in their original condition;

(ii) remain in their original packaging;

(iii) retain original tags or labels, where supplied;

(iv) include all accessories, components and gifts supplied with the purchase; and

(v) were not designated final sale at the time of purchase.

The affected goods and quantities must be identified in the request. Goods returned as part of an approved bundle must include the components specified in that return authorization. A change in preference is not treated as a defect where the goods match the purchased specification.

The conditions for damaged, faulty or incorrect goods, including exceptions to final-sale and original-packaging requirements, are set out in section 2.5.

### 2.5 Final-sale goods and the store-fault exception

Goods marked final sale at the time of purchase are excluded from change-of-mind returns. A product's general category or a label added after purchase does not establish that it was sold as final sale.

Notwithstanding that exclusion, a request concerning verified arrival damage, a defect or an incorrect item may be accepted within the period in section 2.1. In those circumstances, the unused and original-packaging conditions may be waived where opening, testing or loss of packaging was reasonably connected with discovering or reporting the fault. Secure return packaging is still required where the goods are to be returned.

This exception does not cover damage caused by later misuse, extend the thirty-day request period or establish an entitlement to a refund without assessment. Evidence and any required inspection remain applicable. Missing original packaging alone is not a sufficient reason to reject a qualifying damaged-item claim.

### 2.6 Requests outside the ordinary conditions

A request submitted after thirty days will be referred for human review of the stated circumstances. It is not automatically accepted or rejected. Any approved exception must identify the goods and the condition or deadline being varied. Contact with customer service or an unanswered earlier message does not, by itself, establish an approved extension.

Digital goods, services and standalone promotional gifts are outside the standard physical-return process. A claim concerning such a purchase requires human review; absence of a physical-return process does not automatically determine whether a separate refund claim is valid.

## 3. Supporting evidence

### 3.1 Evidence relevant to the request

Customers should provide the order reference, affected goods and quantities, a description of the issue and relevant supporting evidence. Depending on the reason, this may include:

(a) photographs of visible damage, the goods and their packaging;

(b) photographs of the goods received and their identifying labels where an incorrect item is alleged;

(c) photographs or packing information showing the contents of an incomplete parcel; or

(d) a description and, where safe and practical, a demonstration of a malfunction.

Customers must not operate unsafe goods to demonstrate a defect. Non-receipt is assessed using shipment and investigation evidence rather than a photograph of goods that are absent. Passwords, full payment-card details and unrelated personal information are not required.

Where evidence cannot be provided, accessed or safely obtained, the exception in section 3.2 applies.

### 3.2 Unavailable, inaccessible or unsafe evidence

If evidence is unavailable, inaccessible or unsafe to produce, the limitation must be recorded and the request referred for human review instead of automatic rejection. This is an exception to the ordinary evidence requirements in section 3.1.

An uploaded attachment is not automatically accepted as verified evidence. A human reviewer will determine whether the available material is sufficient. Referral for review does not itself approve the return or refund.

### 3.3 Clarification and existing requests

Further relevant information may be requested where the issue, delivery date, quantities or condition remains unclear. Customer statements, inspection findings and confirmed transaction details will be considered separately; a disputed statement is not treated as an established fact merely because it appears in a request.

Additional evidence concerning an existing case should be attached to that case. Repeated submissions for the same goods do not create separate refund entitlements. Any earlier approval remains limited to the goods, quantities and resolution it expressly covered.

## 4. Return procedure

### 4.1 Return authorization

Goods must not be dispatched until a return authorization and return destination have been provided. The authorization identifies the goods, quantities and approved shipping arrangement. Permission to send goods back is not confirmation that inspection has passed or a refund has been processed.

### 4.2 Dispatch deadline

Authorized goods must be handed to the carrier within seven (7) calendar days after approval of the return, unless an extension under section 4.5 applies. The approval date is day zero and the seventh day is included.

Carrier acceptance or a drop-off receipt establishes dispatch. Creation of a label alone does not establish that the goods were sent. The dispatch deadline concerns handover, not arrival at the warehouse; delivery to the warehouse after that deadline does not necessarily mean dispatch was late.

### 4.3 Shipping arrangements and amendments

The return may use an authorized pickup, prepaid label or customer-arranged shipment, as specified for the case. A customer-arranged shipment is reimbursable only under the conditions in section 5.1.

Changes to the method, collection date or return details may be requested before carrier handover and take effect only when confirmed. Once the goods have been handed over, changes are subject to the carrier's handling and are not guaranteed. A pickup or label must not be treated as arranged until confirmation has been issued.

### 4.4 Condition and packaging

Goods must be packed securely to prevent damage or loss during return. The return must include the authorized quantities and the accessories, components and gifts required by the approved resolution. The customer should retain the return reference and proof of handover.

Original packaging remains required for a change-of-mind return. Where section 2.5 applies and original packaging is damaged or unavailable, suitable protective packaging may be used instead. A claim concerning an original fault must not be rejected solely because the original packaging is absent.

### 4.5 Extensions and failed collection

A recorded human-approved extension replaces the ordinary dispatch deadline only for the named return and the new date specified. A failed store-arranged pickup may support a request for an extension but does not create one automatically.

Late or unverified dispatch requires human review. A customer must not be told that the return was automatically cancelled without a recorded cancellation or decision.

### 4.6 Unsafe returns

Leaking goods, unsafe batteries and other goods presenting a handling risk require human review and specific return instructions. Customers should not dispatch such goods using an ordinary return arrangement or dispose of them unless expressly instructed to do so.

## 5. Outbound and return-shipping fees

### 5.1 Cost of return shipment

For a verified store-fault return, the store bears the approved return-shipping cost using the authorized arrangement. Where the customer arranges shipment, reimbursement requires:

(i) prior approval of the method and cost; and

(ii) evidence of the amount actually paid.

For a change-of-mind return, return shipping is normally the customer's responsibility. The applicable charge or responsibility must be explained before handover. Unconfirmed fault or disputed charges require further review before reimbursement is agreed.

### 5.2 Original outbound shipping

The original outbound shipping fee is normally not refunded for a change-of-mind return. Where an approved store-fault resolution affects the whole order, the recorded outbound fee is included once.

For a partial return, any outbound-fee refund depends on a recorded allocation or an expressly approved amount. The whole fee is not automatically refundable for each returned item. For an accepted full cancellation before fulfillment, the refund includes the recorded original shipping and tax, less amounts already refunded or pending refund.

## 6. Refunds

### 6.1 Conditions for payment

A refund is issued after the request has been accepted and, where a physical return is required, the returned goods have been received and the applicable inspection completed. The inspection considers the identity, quantities, completeness and conditions relevant to the original return reason, including any exception in section 2.5.

The store targets inspection within three (3) business days after warehouse receipt. Return tracking marked delivered does not itself establish that inspection is complete. A delay or a disputed finding may be raised for review under section 7.

Physical return may be waived following human review where non-delivery is confirmed or return is unsafe or impractical. The waiver applies only to the goods and resolution specified. It does not remove the requirement to establish the payment and refundable amount.

### 6.2 Refund amount

The amount is based on the approved affected quantities and the amount paid for those goods, including the applicable recorded discount and tax allocations. It is not calculated using a later catalog price. Shipping components for returns are governed by section 5 of this Policy. For an accepted full cancellation before fulfillment, the recorded original shipping and tax are included, subject to the captured balance and earlier refunds.

A partial return does not entitle the customer to reimbursement of retained or unaffected goods. Where the value of an accessory, a discount allocation or another component cannot be established, the amount requires review rather than an assumed allocation.

### 6.3 Payment destination and previous refunds

Refunds are made in SGD to the original payment method. They cannot be redirected to a different account or payment instrument supplied during a support conversation.

The total of completed and pending refunds must not exceed the amount captured for the order. Amounts already refunded or reserved for an existing refund will be deducted from the remaining balance. Failed payments and uncaptured authorizations cannot be refunded as settled payments. Approval of a return does not authorize a duplicate refund.

### 6.4 Refund timeframe

The store targets refund initiation within two (2) business days after final acceptance and completion of the required receipt and inspection, or an approved waiver. Return authorization alone does not start this period.

After processing, supported card and PayNow refunds normally appear within five (5) to ten (10) business days. These are estimated periods rather than guaranteed posting dates. Requested, accepted and pending refunds may not yet have been processed.

### 6.5 Delayed refunds and other resolutions

Where the relevant period has passed or the recorded status conflicts with the payment received, the customer may request further review. An additional refund will not be issued solely because an earlier refund is not yet visible.

Exchange, replacement, repair, store credit and goodwill compensation require separate agreement. A request for one of these alternatives does not guarantee stock, dispatch or payment.

## 7. Return-parcel disputes

### 7.1 Grounds for a dispute

A dispute may be raised concerning:

(a) a return parcel reported as not received;

(b) missing goods, quantities, accessories or bundle components;

(c) a different item in the returned parcel;

(d) damage, a fault or a condition inconsistent with the accepted return reason;

(e) the authenticity of purchased or returned goods; or

(f) whether a change-of-mind return meets the applicable condition requirements.

A dispute initiates a review. It does not establish that either party's account is correct or create a second refund entitlement.

### 7.2 Incomplete, damaged, faulty or wrong goods

The original return reason, purchase details, supporting evidence and inspection findings will be considered together. Review must distinguish the original reported fault from an allegation of additional damage, missing contents or a different item during the return.

The customer is not automatically responsible for damage merely because the goods were returned damaged. A qualifying original fault remains subject to section 2.5. Conflicting accounts or inspection findings will be referred for human review rather than automatic refund or rejection.

### 7.3 Counterfeit products

A claim concerning authenticity will be referred for human review. Relevant product, packaging and purchase evidence may be requested. A brand name, low price, image or allegation alone does not establish that goods are genuine or counterfeit.

Any accepted resolution will identify the affected goods, whether physical return is required and the applicable refund. No special compensation or determination of authenticity is implied merely by raising the claim.

### 7.4 Goods returned due to change of mind

A dispute may arise where inspection reports use, missing original packaging or tags, missing accessories, an incomplete bundle or a different item. The applicable conditions are those in section 2.4 and the agreed return authorization.

If the customer contests the finding, the request will be referred for human review. Where the original reason concerns arrival damage, a defect or an incorrect item, section 2.5 must be considered rather than applying change-of-mind conditions without that exception.

### 7.5 Review and communication

The store may request further relevant evidence and will communicate the reviewed outcome and its reason. An unresolved allegation is not proof of misuse, fraud or ineligibility. Unavailable or unsafe evidence is handled under section 3.2.

A customer may request human review of a disputed decision. Existing case details and additional evidence should remain associated with the same request. Contact with support is not, by itself, confirmation that an investigation, amendment or payment has been completed.
